NFC

Lets the app read or emulate NFC tags and cards.

Asks you firstHardware and accessoriesNFCAndroid and iPhoneWhile you use it

In one line

Can scan NFC tags and cards that you hold to the phone

Lets the app read or emulate NFC tags and cards.

Reads tags, payment-style cards and badges. Android grants it at install; iOS uses a capability and purpose string.

What it can and can't do

Reach 2 of 4Tags you hold close

What the user does: You tap Scan tag and hold the phone near an NFC tag or card

It can

  • Scan tags, badges and similar cards
  • Scan tags in the background on iPhone (iOS 12 and later)
  • Act as a contactless card on Android

It cannot

  • Scan on iPhone without the system scan sheet for standard scanning
  • Act as a card on iPhone in most regions
  • Work on iPhone 6 and older; scanning starts with iPhone 7

How it differs between platforms: Android grants NFC at install with no prompt; iPhone shows a system scan sheet and limits card emulation by region.

Technical limits

  • iOS uses a system scan sheet
  • Card emulation limited on iOS
  • iOS shows a system scan sheet; Android reads silently while the app is on screen
  • Android 10+ blocks reading from the background
  • Needs touch distance of a few centimeters

The story

What it is

Lets an app read tags and cards you tap on the phone, and sometimes act as a card.

For you

You hold the phone near a tag on a poster, a smart card, a door or a product. The phone buzzes and the app opens. It usually needs no prompt on Android and a system sheet on iPhone.

For makers

A tap is the fastest way to start a task, such as paying, checking in or pairing. Makers use tags for marketing, access control and proof that an item is genuine. A tag can start an app with a link.

Risks

  • Rogue tags can open links
  • Skimming of cards
  • Tracking by tag

Android and iPhone in short

  • Android: Android grants NFC at install and has supported emulating a card since Android 4.4.
  • iPhone: Reader mode works on iPhone 7 and later, and background tag reading works since iOS 12. Third party card emulation is limited by region.

What the platform says

  • Apple: Core NFC. Read NFC tags in your app, including in the background, for fast tap based experiences.
  • Google: NFC on Android. Use NFC to read tags and talk to cards from your app.

A real example: LinkedIn

LinkedIn passport verification, run with Persona, needs the LinkedIn mobile app, the phone's NFC scanner and the camera for the photo page and a live selfie. Persona deletes face geometry right after processing and keeps ID and selfie for up to 30 days (third-party write-ups).

History

  • 2010-12, Android 2.3 (API 9): NFC reading and writing
  • 2013-10, Android 4.4 (API 19): Host card emulation
  • 2017-09, iPhone iOS 11: Core NFC introduced
  • 2017-09, iPhone iOS 11: Core NFC NDEF reading on iPhone 7
  • 2018-09, iPhone iOS 12: Background tag reading on iPhone XS and later
  • 2019-09, iPhone iOS 13: Tag writing and native tag access
  • 2019-09, iPhone iOS 13: Core NFC tag reading and ISO 7816 added
  • 2020-09, iPhone iOS 14: Passport chip reading support improved

Related capabilities

Related

  • Contactless payments and NFC secure element. Can act as a contactless card or key on Android freely, but iPhone needs Apple approval
  • Host card emulation (HCE). Can act as a contactless card or key when you pick it as default
  • Secure Element and NFC platform access. Can use the phone's secure chip only with special approval
  • Request an ID or credential from a wallet (verifier). Can ask your wallet to prove one fact, such as your age, once you approve
  • Camera. Can use your camera while the app is open, after you tap Allow
  • LinkedIn ID verification (CLEAR, Persona). Can check a government ID and a selfie through a partner inside the app

Same idea on the web

  • Web NFC. Can scan NFC tags or save to them with your phone, after you allow it
  • Web NFC. Can scan NFC tags and save to them when your phone touches one
