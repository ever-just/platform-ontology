Secure Element and NFC platform access

Reads and writes applets in the phone's secure chip for keys, badges and payments.

Works without askingHardware and accessoriesNFCAndroid and iPhoneWhile you use it

In one line

Can use the phone's secure chip only with special approval

Reads and writes applets in the phone's secure chip for keys, badges and payments.

The secure element holds the strongest credentials on the phone. Third-party access means banks, car makers and employers can issue keys without Apple or Google Wallet.

What it can and can't do

Reach 3 of 4Ongoing, but approval gated

What the user does: You add a car key, work badge or bank credential from the issuer's app

It can

  • Store keys, badges and payment credentials on the secure chip
  • Let banks, car makers and employers issue keys without Apple or Google Wallet
  • Run on iPhone in selected countries and the EEA

It cannot

  • Use the chip on iPhone without Apple's entitlement, agreement and fee
  • Use the chip on Android without the chip vendor's access rules
  • Be used by most Android apps, which use host card emulation instead

How it differs between platforms: iPhone needs an Apple entitlement and fee in selected countries; Android needs access rules from the chip vendor, so wallets and carriers mostly use it.

The story

What it is

Lets an approved app store and run keys, badges and payment cards inside the phone's tamper proof chip.

For you

You tap your phone on a hotel door, a car or a payment reader and it works as fast as a card. The key is held in a locked chip, so someone who copies your app cannot copy the key.

For makers

The chip is the safest place on a phone for car keys, bank cards and access badges. Banks, car makers and employers want to use it for their own brand. Platforms limit it to vetted companies and regions.

Risks

  • Locked to a few vendors
  • Lost phone could open doors
  • Rules differ by country

Android and iPhone in short

  • Android: Most Android apps use software card emulation. Third party secure chip access needs rules set by the chip owner.
  • iPhone: Apple offers it in selected countries (including Australia, Brazil, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, the UK and the US) and in the EEA under European law.

What the platform says

  • Apple: NFC and SE Platform. Approved apps can use the phone's secure element for tap payments, car keys, IDs and more.

Good uses and abuse

Legitimate uses

  • Car keys
  • Hotel keys
  • Corporate badges
  • Student IDs
