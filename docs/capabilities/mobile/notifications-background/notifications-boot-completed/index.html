In one line

Can start itself in the background after your Android phone restarts

Lets the app start in the background after the phone reboots.

Lets apps resume tasks after reboot. Android 15 restricts launching certain foreground services from boot receivers.

What it can and can't do

Reach 3 of 4Starts after every restart

What the user does: Nothing. It happens when you restart the phone

It can

  • Start up again after a reboot to restore alarms, syncs or reminders
  • Run light setup work once the phone is on
  • Be allowed at install without a prompt

It cannot

  • Start some foreground services at boot; Android 15 blocks types like media, camera and microphone
  • Do this on iPhone, where apps do not run at boot
  • Show anything at boot without notifications allowed

How it differs between platforms: Android lets apps start after boot. iPhone only wakes apps through push, location events or background tasks.

The story

What it is

Lets an Android app start by itself after the phone restarts.

For you

You restart your phone and your alarms, reminders and sync work again without you opening anything. It is a quiet convenience.

For makers

Makers use it to bring back timers and background tasks after a reboot. It is also how unwanted apps stay running, so Android 15 blocks some types from starting this way.

Risks

  • Keeps unwanted apps running
  • Hard to see

Android and iPhone in short

  • Android: Granted at install. Android 15 blocks some foreground service types from boot.
  • iPhone: iOS apps cannot start at boot. Only push, location events or tasks can wake them.

Good uses and abuse

Legitimate uses

  • Alarm and sync restoration

Known abuse

  • Persistence of unwanted apps
