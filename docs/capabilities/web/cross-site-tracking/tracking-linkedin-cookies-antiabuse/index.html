LinkedIn anti-abuse cookies (li_bapfcc, li_odapfcc, li_apfcdc, spectroscopyId, fid, fcookie)

Cookies that control and carry the fingerprint and extension scans, disclosed in LinkedIn's own table.

Used to track youTracking across apps and sitesCookiesWeb browsersWhen the page loadsWhile you use itWhen you are signed in

In one line

Can check your browser for bots and add a device fingerprint to your visit

Cookies that control and carry the fingerprint and extension scans, disclosed in LinkedIn's own table.

First party confirmation that LinkedIn collects 'abuse prevention features' from the member's device and checks for extensions. The Cookie Policy text itself does not mention fingerprinting or extensions.

What it can and can't do

Reach 3 of 4Your browser's traits, each visit

What the user does: Nothing. It happens when a LinkedIn page loads

It can

  • Store small markers in your browser for bot and abuse checks
  • Carry a device fingerprint with your visits
  • Record the result of extension scans LinkedIn discloses in its cookie table

It cannot

  • Work if you clear the cookies, though a fingerprint may rebuild
  • Name you without a LinkedIn login
  • See other tabs or your files
  • Be turned off with the normal cookie banner for essential security

The story

What it is

Cookies tied to LinkedIn's checks on your device and browser extensions, listed in its own cookie table.

For you

You never see these cookies or the checks behind them. LinkedIn's own cookie table says they support abuse prevention. A public analysis says the related scripts look at the browser and add ons on your device.

For makers

Fake accounts and scrapers are a big cost for a social site. Scanning for browser extensions and device traits helps LinkedIn tell bots from people. The same data can act as a device fingerprint, so critics ask whether it is used for more than safety.

A real example: LinkedIn

LinkedIn's cookie table describes li_bapfcc and li_odapfcc as controlling the number of abuse prevention features collected from the member's device, li_apfcdc as triggering them, spectroscopyId as catching malicious activity through browser extensions.

Related capabilities

Related

  • APFC / DNA device fingerprint (abuse prevention features). Can collect about 48 device traits and send them in secret with later requests
  • Active extension probe (AED). Can check which of thousands of known browser extensions you have installed
  • HUMAN Security (PerimeterX) hidden iframe. Can run a hidden bot check on LinkedIn pages and tie it to its browser ID
