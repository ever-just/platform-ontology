Idle Detection

Tells a site when the person is away from keyboard and mouse or the screen is locked.

Asks you firstNotifications and backgroundKeep awakeWeb browsersWhile you use it

In one line

Can tell when you step away or lock your screen, if you allow it

Tells a site when the person is away from keyboard and mouse or the screen is locked.

Reveals when the person is present: active or idle (at least 60 seconds) and whether the screen is locked. Useful for chat presence; also shows daily schedule, sleep and work hours.

What it can and can't do

Reach 2 of 4While open, if you allow

What the user does: You tap a button like Show my status and tap Allow

It can

  • See whether you are active or idle for at least 60 seconds
  • See if your screen is locked
  • Show presence in chat or reset a shared kiosk
  • Keep checking while the page is open

It cannot

  • Work before you tap Allow in Chrome
  • Work in Firefox, Safari or on iPhone
  • Work on Samsung Internet
  • See what you are doing, only whether you are active or away

How it differs between platforms: Chrome and Edge on computers and Android ask first. Firefox, Safari and iPhone browsers do not support it.

The story

What it is

A site can learn when you step away from the keyboard and mouse or lock your screen.

For you

A prompt asks to know when you are idle. If you allow it, the site can show you as away in chat or pause things. You never see the checks themselves.

For makers

Makers use it to show presence in chat and team tools, and to save resources. It is also a window into when you are at your desk. That is why it needs a clear allow.

Risks

  • Shows when you are away
  • Can feel like spying
